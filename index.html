Seal files

Every file becomes a leaf of a Merkle tree and one 32-byte root seals the set. You get a seal.json with each file's hash and inclusion proof. Keep it somewhere the files themselves cannot reach.

Attack lab

Two well-known ways to forge a Merkle root. Each one runs for real in your browser, first against a deliberately weakened tree and then against the tree Merkle Seal uses. Switch the defence on and off to compare.

Signing key

A root proves the files have not changed, not who sealed them. Signing it with an ECDSA P-256 key adds that. Seals you make in this tab are signed with the active key, and verifiers see its fingerprint.
